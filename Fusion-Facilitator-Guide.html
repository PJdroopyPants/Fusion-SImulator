Booth Facilitator Guide

Fusion Power Generator

A one-page run-of-show for the volunteer staffing the exhibit. You do not need to know fusion physics; the simulator explains itself. Your job is to invite people in and let them drive.

1Set it up (2 minutes)

  1. Open the exhibit: double-click index.html in the folder. It runs offline in any modern browser, no internet needed.
  2. Go fullscreen: press Present (top right). This enlarges the reactor and the headline numbers and hides the denser panels (the booth view). When nobody is touching it, an auto demo cycles through the presets; the moment a visitor touches anything the demo backs off, and it shows a countdown before taking over again.
  3. Leave it on Cruise. It opens on a healthy, net-positive reactor. If no one touches it for a few minutes it gently resets itself, so it is always inviting.
  4. Sound is off by default. Turn it on with the Sound button only if the room is quiet; keep the volume low.
  5. Tablet kiosk (optional): "Add to Home Screen" launches it fullscreen with no browser bars.

2The 30-second invite

A script you can read to anyone walking past. Tap Startup first, so the visitor starts from a struggling reactor and personally turns it around.

“Want to run a fusion reactor? This is the same kind of machine they're building to make power from seawater. Right now it's losing power: see Net electric up top, in red? Slide the temperature up… now give the magnets more field… there: it flipped green. You're sending real power to the grid. Think you can reach ignition?”

Watch Net electric in the top strip (red to green), not the gold Q number. If they stall, the coach line under the reactor says what to fix next.

3What to point at

  • The presets (Startup → Cruise → High Gain) jump to whole scenarios, great for a quick wow.
  • The three big numbers up top: state, net electric, and energy gain Q. Green is good.
  • High Gain reaches Ignition, and the core flares white-hot. That is the moment people remember.
  • Challenges (the chip up top) and the Guided story button keep curious visitors busy longer.
  • The 60s Challenge (top strip) is the queue-builder: 60 seconds, no presets, and the most net power held stable wins; best of the day goes on the board. Try “think you can beat the top score?”

5Questions people actually ask

They askYou say
“Is this a real reactor design?”It's a physically grounded teaching model of a tokamak like ITER. The trends and tradeoffs are real; the exact numbers are illustrative.
“Why hasn't anyone done this yet?”Holding a 150-million-degree plasma steady takes enormous magnets and decades of engineering. The record for a magnetic machine like this one is about two-thirds of breakeven (JET, 1997); a laser experiment (NIF) first crossed breakeven in 2022, a different approach. Point at the ignition bar under the reactor, or exit Present mode to show the operating-point map: the diamonds are real machines, and none has crossed the ignition curve.
“When will fusion power homes?”ITER is being built to demonstrate a tenfold energy gain, with its full-power fuel runs starting around 2039; serious roadmaps put the first power plants in the 2040s. Private ventures are racing to beat that.
“Where does the fuel come from?”Deuterium comes from seawater. Tritium barely exists in nature, so the plant breeds its own from lithium in the blanket: that's the “Tritium breeding” readout.
“Is it dangerous?”There's no chain reaction to run away. Any upset and the plasma fizzles out in about a second: that's the disruption you can trigger yourself with the Stress preset.

6Good to know

Keyboard-friendly arrow keys orbit the reactor, +/− zoom. Themes a Light theme reads better on a projector in a bright room; High contrast and Colorblind options are in the Theme menu. Plain words switches the lessons to plain language for younger visitors. Real-world units shows temperature in millions of degrees and power in homes powered.

This is a teaching model: the numbers are illustrative and tuned for intuition, not engineering accuracy. If a visitor asks a question you can't answer, that's a great thing; point them to the program's table or website.
