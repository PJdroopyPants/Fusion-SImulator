Enhancement Roadmap · Prepared for the Tokamak Learning Lab

Fusion Power Generator Simulator

A prioritized set of ideas to push the visuals and the teaching value further, scoped for a donation to the Rose-Hulman and Purdue fusion outreach programs and an audience that runs from curious high schoolers to engineering undergraduates.

00

What you are starting from

Worth saying plainly, because the roadmap builds on it rather than replacing it: this is already a serious piece of work. The recommendations below are additive.

  • A genuine dependency-free 3D engine on a 2D canvas: perspective camera, painter's depth sort, atmospheric fog, additive bloom, vignette.
  • A physically motivated model: triple product, a real Lawson ignition curve, energy gain Q, power balance, breeding ratio.
  • Six live data views, including a Lawson operating-point map, a Sankey energy flow, and an animated D-T fuel cycle.
  • A mission system with seven constraint puzzles that force real tradeoffs.
  • Teachable tooltips, expandable lessons, a glossary, and a contextual operator coach.
  • A full navigation HUD: orientation gizmo, view snaps, camera cluster, reduced-motion support.

The strategic call

The zero-dependency design is itself an asset for a donation: it runs offline at a booth by opening one file, and a student can maintain it. The roadmap therefore keeps that core intact and treats a WebGL rewrite as an optional, clearly-scoped track rather than the default path.

03

Main canvas: the reactor

The 3D stage is the first thing anyone sees and the thing they will photograph. These ideas raise the spectacle and, just as importantly, make the physics legible inside the picture itself.

Make fusion actually happen on screen

A1

Right now ions stream around the torus but never react. Spawn real fusion events in the hot core: when fuel ions meet, emit a bright flash, a gold alpha that stays and spirals, and a fast neutron that fires outward. Rate scales with fusion power, so the core visibly "boils" harder as you climb toward ignition.

This is the single most motivating canvas change. It turns an abstract glow into a story a 12-year-old reads instantly: that is the reaction, and that is its energy leaving.

Quick winEffort - LowHigh impactAll

Code hook: you already declare sparks = [] in seedParticles() and never use it. Populate it on a probabilistic core collision, tie spawn rate to model.fusionPower, and reuse the existing neutrons render path for the ejecta.

A living plasma and a richer bloom

A2

Modulate the core brightness with cheap layered noise so it churns and flickers like a real burning plasma instead of a steady lamp. Pair it with a two or three step threshold bloom (bright-pass, then blur at a couple of scales) for the star-like halo that sells "hotter than the Sun."

Both are screen-space and stay inside your current pipeline. The plasma reads as alive even when the controls are untouched.

Quick winEffort - LowLooks premiumAll

Code hook: extend applyBloom() (currently a single downscaled pass) to a bright-pass plus multi-scale blur, and drive plasmaColor() intensity with a time-based noise term.

Cutaway / cross-section mode

A3

A toggle that slices the torus on a plane and reveals the nested shells the way a textbook does: plasma core, separatrix, breeding blanket, vacuum vessel, then the coils caging it all. Label each layer in place. Students finally see that the machine is shells within shells, not a solid donut.

This is the highest teaching-per-pixel idea on the canvas. It answers "what is it actually made of" without a single slide.

High impactEffort - MedTop teaching valueAll

Code hook: in the painter's pass, cull primitives on one side of a world-space plane (drop any whose projected point sits beyond the cut), and draw rim rings where each shell meets the plane. Your geometry is already parametric, so the cut is a depth test, not new models.

Neutron wall-heat map

A4

Where neutrons strike the blanket, accumulate a heat signature that warms the vessel ribs from amber to white and cools when the divertor sweep is on. The abstract "wall load 8 MW/m squared" readout becomes a glowing surface you can point at.

It also makes the divertor toggle feel consequential, because you watch the peak load spread and fade.

Quick winEffort - LowTies UI to 3DUG

Code hook: accumulate a per-rib intensity where the existing neutrons terminate, color the vessel loop strokes by it, and relax it toward zero each frame scaled by model.wallLoad and divertorSweep.

Give it human scale

A5

Drop a one-meter scale bar and a small human silhouette beside the vessel, with an optional school-bus outline for the whole plant. Almost no one realizes a tokamak is the size of a building. A single figure standing next to it reframes the entire image.

Quick winEffort - LowMemorableHS

Code hook: add a couple of billboarded primitives at fixed world coordinates near the vessel, faded by depthBright() like your hotspots, behind a "Scale" toggle.

Field lines and ions that teach

A6

Animate a flowing dash along the helical field lines so direction of the winding is obvious, and density that thickens with B. Color ions by species: deuterium, tritium, the alpha ash, electrons. The screen stops being decorative motion and starts being a particle key.

Quick winEffort - LowClarifies physicsUG

Code hook: add a lineDashOffset march to the field-line strokes, and give each entry in particles a species field that picks both color and size.

Stand it in a room

A7

The plant currently floats in black. A subtle reflective floor grid, a soft horizon gradient, and faint contact reflections under the reactor give a sense of place and depth without stealing focus. It reads as a facility, not a logo.

High impactEffort - MedProduction polishAll

Code hook: you already draw a ground pad and contact shadows in addBOP(). Extend that to a larger projected grid plane and a mirrored, dimmed re-draw of the nearest solids.

Show a disruption, do not just label it

A8

When pressure outruns the field, make it visceral: the plasma kinks and wobbles, field lines tangle, a bright energy dump flashes onto the wall, and the stage shudders before the state pill turns red. Failure becomes the most memorable thing a visitor sees, and it is a safe way to teach why control matters.

High impactEffort - MedUnforgettableAll

Code hook: when model.disruptionRisk crosses threshold, add a transient wobble to the torus poloidal angle, a white wall flash, and a brief canvas shake, gated by your reduceMotion flag.

The big bet: a WebGL render track

A9

For the largest pure-visual leap, add an optional renderer using a single vendored Three.js file: a true depth buffer with no painter's artifacts, a raymarched volumetric plasma, thousands of GPU particles, physically based metal coils, real HDR bloom, and shadows. Keep the current canvas engine as the default so the offline, zero-dependency promise holds.

Recommended as a parallel "v2" you can switch to, not a replacement. The honest tradeoff: much higher ceiling, materially more code to maintain.

AmbitiousEffort - HighCeiling raiseAll

Code hook: wrap the renderer behind a flag so drawReactor() and a new drawReactorGL() are interchangeable; ship three.min.js in the folder to stay portable for booths with no network.

04

The interface around it

Outreach is a hostile environment for software: bright rooms, projectors, touch screens, and visitors who will not read a manual. These changes make the UI survive a booth and look polished doing it.

Presentation / kiosk mode

B1

One toggle that goes fullscreen, enlarges the reactor and the three headline numbers, hides the denser developer-facing panels, and idles into a gentle auto-demo when no one touches it. This is the difference between a tool and an exhibit.

Quick winEffort - LowBooth-readyAll

Code hook: a body.kiosk class plus the Fullscreen API; reuse your existing auto-spin idle timer to trigger a scripted preset cycle.

Themes: light, high-contrast, colorblind-safe

B2

Dark looks great on a laptop and can wash out on a projector in a lit gym. Ship a light theme, a high-contrast theme, and a colorblind-safe palette so the teal, gold, and coral stay distinguishable. Your CSS is already fully variable-driven, so this is mostly new variable sets.

Quick winEffort - LowAccessibilityAll

Code hook: the :root custom properties in styles.css are the whole theme. Swap them under [data-theme] and remember the choice (a tiny preference store).

Numbers that move

B3

Metrics currently snap to new values. Tween them so they count up and down, and let a meaningful crossing (going net-positive, reaching breakeven) pulse briefly. A dashboard that animates feels alive and draws the eye to what just changed.

Quick winEffort - LowFeels aliveAll

Code hook: interpolate displayed values toward model targets inside the existing animation loop rather than writing them directly in updateReadouts().

A guided tour and a power-up intro

B4

On first load, run a short reactor power-up sequence, then a four or five step coached tour: click Cruise, now raise temperature, watch the operating point climb. It converts a wall of unfamiliar controls into a friendly on-ramp for unattended visitors.

High impactEffort - MedSelf-serveAll

Code hook: extend the existing navLegend dismiss logic into a step sequence that spotlights one control at a time.

A control-room annunciator strip

B5

Add a row of status lamps that light and softly blink on the conditions you already compute: disruption risk, thermal limit, breeding below one, ignition achieved. It deepens the control-room fantasy and teaches operational awareness, reading a board at a glance, which is its own engineering skill.

Quick winEffort - LowOn-themeAll

Code hook: the conditions are already branched in calculateModel() for the state label; surface those same booleans as lamps.

Touch and tablet polish

B6

Booths run on tablets. The sliders and tooltip dots are small for fingers. Enlarge touch targets, add plus and minus steppers beside each slider, and make the info dots tap-friendly. The orbit gestures already work on touch, so this is the last mile to a true kiosk.

High impactEffort - MedReachAll

Code hook: larger hit areas on .control input and .tip-dot, plus optional stepper buttons that nudge the same range inputs.

An optional reactor soundscape

B7

A low hum that rises in pitch with fusion power, a soft alarm on disruption, and a chime at ignition or mission complete. Sound is disproportionately effective at a noisy event for pulling people to the table. Must be muted by default and one click to enable.

High impactEffort - MedDraws a crowdAll

Code hook: a small Web Audio oscillator whose frequency tracks model.fusionPower; trigger one-shots from the same mission and state transitions you already detect. No audio code exists yet, so this is clean to add.

Micro-illustrations on the controls

B8

A tiny inline icon by each setpoint, a coil, a fuel pellet, a thermometer, a turbine, helps younger and non-native-English visitors parse the panel without reading every label. Small touch, large gain in approachability.

Quick winEffort - LowApproachableHS

Code hook: inline SVG in each .control heading, matching the stroke style you already use in the camera cluster buttons.

05

Teaching value and new features

The sim already teaches by letting people drive. These add structure for those who want it, depth for those ready for it, and motivation for everyone, without turning it into homework.

Guided story mode

C1

A narrated path from cold gas to a self-sustaining burn. Each step unlocks one control and explains it: first heat the fuel, now hold it with the field, now feed it, now catch the energy. Five minutes, and a visitor leaves understanding the whole chain rather than having wiggled some sliders.

Free play stays the default. Story mode is the on-ramp for the many who want to be led.

High impactEffort - MedBest for HS / publicHS

Code hook: your lessons object and MISSIONS array are already the content spine; sequence them with a "next" control and a soft spotlight.

Real machines, and where you stand

C2

Preset buttons that load the operating regime of real devices and plot them on your Lawson map next to the visitor's point: ITER, SPARC, JET, EAST, with NIF shown as the contrasting laser approach. The payoff line writes itself: here is where you got the plasma, and here is where ITER is aiming.

It makes the sim current and credible, and it is the natural bridge to "these are real projects you could work on."

Quick winEffort - LowCurrent + credibleAll

Code hook: add named points to the drawLawson() overlay and a preset row beside your existing one. Section 06 has vetted 2024 to 2025 numbers to seed it.

Real-world unit translators

C3

A toggle that re-expresses the abstract readouts in human terms: temperature as "X times the Sun's core," net power as "homes powered," fuel as "grams of seawater deuterium per day." The physics stays; the meaning lands for a general audience.

Quick winEffort - LowMakes it tangibleHS

Code hook: a formatting layer over the same model values feeding updateReadouts(); no model changes needed.

Make it move through time

C4

The model is quasi-static: it jumps straight to equilibrium. Add light time dynamics so temperature and density ramp with thermal inertia, the burn can run away into ignition or slide into a quench over a few seconds, and the history charts show real transients. This is the biggest jump in physical realism and it makes ignition feel earned.

High impactEffort - MedDeeper physicsUG

Code hook: evolve a small state vector (stored energy, density) toward the targets calculateModel() computes, integrated in the existing 360 ms tick, instead of displaying equilibrium instantly.

Scenario challenges and an operator exam

C5

Extend the mission system into timed scenarios with events: a sudden density drop, a partial coil quench, a cooling fault. The student has to diagnose and recover. For undergraduates this turns the toy into a genuine control exercise and pairs naturally with a quench-and-recover lab.

High impactEffort - MedRigor for UGUG

Code hook: builds directly on MISSIONS and updateMissions(); add scripted disturbances that perturb the inputs on a timer.

Predict, then test

C6

Short inline prompts that ask before they let you act: "If you raise the field, what happens to Q?" The visitor guesses, then changes the slider and sees. Prediction-first is one of the most effective teaching patterns there is, and the sim is the perfect answer key.

Quick winEffort - LowProven pedagogyHS

Code hook: a small prompt component that reads the relevant model field before and after a change to confirm the prediction.

Export the run as a lab worksheet

C7

Let a student export their operating point and history as a CSV or a one-page report: setpoints, triple product, Q, net power. It plugs the simulator straight into a classroom assignment and matches a hands-on, lab-report teaching style well.

High impactEffort - MedClassroom-readyUG

Code hook: you already keep netHistory, fusionHistory, and trail; serialize them to a Blob download. No backend required.

A "why fusion" context panel

C8

A short, optional framing module: the fuel comes from seawater and lithium, the energy density dwarfs chemical fuels, there is no long-lived high-level waste stream like fission, and it cannot melt down. For a public audience this is the motivation that makes the rest worth caring about.

Quick winEffort - LowThe "so what"HS

Code hook: a new collapsible panel in the learning row, same pattern as your glossary and lesson panels.

A reading-level switch

C9

One control that swaps the wording of lessons, the coach, and tooltips between plain language and technical. The same simulator then serves a middle-schooler and a junior engineering student without dumbing down for either. It is how one tool covers your whole audience range.

High impactEffort - MedCovers all agesAll

Code hook: give the lessons, TIPS, and coach strings two variants keyed by a level flag.

Step through the energy ledger

C10

Your Sankey is excellent. Add a step-through that pauses on each conversion, heat to thermal, thermal to gross, minus recirculating, and annotates it with the governing relation and the efficiency at that stage. It turns a static diagram into a guided derivation of net electric.

High impactEffort - MedCloses the loopUG

Code hook: stage the ribbon draws in drawSankey() behind a step index, each with a caption from the same intermediate values you already compute.

Real-world and program tie-ins

A donated tool lands harder when it points back at the people receiving it and at the real machines in the news. This section is ready-to-use content, not just ideas.

Seed data for the "real machines" feature (C2)

Vetted against current sources, mid-2026. Use these to place named points on the Lawson map and to write the comparison copy.

JET (UK, retired 2023)Magnetic, tokamakHeld the magnetic-fusion energy record: 69 MJ released over a ~5 s pulse in its final deuterium-tritium campaign. Q of roughly 0.3.

Purdue: make materials the hero

D1

Purdue's fusion work centers on plasma-material interaction, how the hot edge erodes and reshapes the first wall, the exact problem your blanket and divertor already model. Add a "first wall" layer: a tungsten divertor view, a neutron-damage meter that accrues over a run, and a note that surviving these fluxes is one of the field's defining challenges.

It connects the prettiest failure mode in the sim to the actual research a Purdue visitor might join.

High impactEffort - MedPurdue-alignedUG

Pairs with: the neutron wall-heat map (A4) and disruption event (A8). The physics is already half-built in wallLoad and the divertor logic.

Rose-Hulman: ship it as a lab module

D2

Rose-Hulman is undergraduate-first and hands-on, with a nuclear physics and radiation course that already covers fusion and reactors. Package the sim with a short worksheet and the data export (C7) so it drops into a lab period: derive the Lawson point, hit the missions, export your run, write it up. The reading-level switch (C9) lets the same file serve a campus open house and a sophomore lab.

High impactEffort - MedCurriculum fitUG

Pairs with: data export (C7), predict-then-test (C6), and the energy-ledger walkthrough (C10).

A takeaway card with a QR

D3

When a visitor reaches a milestone, offer a printable or on-screen card: "I reached Q = X at the fusion booth," with a QR to the Rose-Hulman or Purdue fusion pages. It turns a 90-second interaction into a follow-up, which is the whole point of outreach.

Quick winEffort - LowRecruiting hookAll

Code hook: render the card to an offscreen canvas from the current model and offer it as an image download.

A credits and donation panel

D4

A small "about this exhibit" panel that names the donation to both programs, with room for their logos and links, plus a one-line model disclaimer. It makes the gift feel official and gives the hosting program a sense of ownership.

Quick winEffort - LowMakes it a giftAll

Code hook: a footer or modal; your disclaimer copy is a good starting seed.

07

A suggested order of attack

If it were mine to sequence, I would chase visible wins first to build momentum, then the features that deepen the teaching, and treat the rendering rewrite as an optional finale.

Phase 1A weekend

The quick wins that change the first impression: fusion events (A1), multi-pass bloom and living plasma (A2), scale reference (A5), presentation mode (B1), themes (B2), real-machine presets (C2), and unit translators (C3). Mostly hours each, all inside the current engine.

Phase 2A few weeks

The depth: cutaway mode (A3), the disruption event (A8), guided story mode (C1), transient time dynamics (C4), the materials layer (D1), data export (C7), the guided tour (B4), and the soundscape (B7). This is where it becomes a teaching instrument, not just a demo.

Phase 3When you want the ceiling

The big bets: the WebGL render track (A9), two-player race-to-ignition, and a WebXR booth mode. High effort, high reward, and entirely optional. The sim is already excellent without them.

Three things to protect along the way

Portability: keep the default build openable from one folder with no network, so it always works at a booth. Accessibility: honor the reduced-motion path you already have, and keep the new colorblind and high-contrast themes first-class. Performance: the canvas redraws every frame, so gate heavy additions behind the same idle and reduced-motion checks you use today.
