Fusion Power Generator: Lab

Drive a magnetic-confinement fusion reactor toward ignition, read its instruments, and connect what you see to the physics of fusion energy.

Audience: grades 9–14 (high school through lower-undergraduate). Time: 30–45 min. Aligns with NGSS HS-PS1, HS-PS3 (energy), and HS-ESS3 (resources). No prior fusion background needed.

3The Lawson operating point

  1. Record the triple product n·T·τE: ×10²¹ keV·s·m⁻³

  3. On the map, is your operating point below, on, or above the ignition curve?

Raise magnetic field and plasma temperature together and watch the point move. Which direction does it travel on the map, and why does stronger confinement help?

7Going further (short answer)

a. Roughly 80% of D-T fusion energy leaves as neutrons and 20% as alpha particles. Which fraction heats the plasma, and which makes the electricity? Why does this matter for reaching ignition?

b. What is the tritium breeding ratio, and why must a real power plant keep it above 1.0?

c. A disruption ends a shot suddenly. In your own words, what causes one in this model, and how did you avoid it?
