Fusion Power Generator: Answer Key

Instructor copy: do not distribute

Pass-conditions, expected behavior, and model answers for the student lab worksheet. The model is calibrated for intuition; accept reasoning over exact numbers, which drift a little as the plasma settles over a few seconds.

Audience: grades 9–14. Time: 30–45 min lab. Aligns with NGSS HS-PS1, HS-PS3 (energy), and HS-ESS3 (resources).

2Preset behavior (expected)

Approximate, once each preset settles. Exact values vary slightly with the burn-through dynamics.

PresetWhat students should see
StartupJust past scientific breakeven (Q ≈ 1.5; state reads “Breakeven”), but net electric is negative (≈ −250 to −270 MW): fusion power ≈ 240 MW, yet the plant draws more than it makes. Good illustration that Q > 1 is not the same as net power.
CruiseA strong burning plasma that goes net positive (state reads “Net Power”), with a high gain; Q settles around 15–20 once the burn-through ramps (net electric ≈ +300 MW).
High GainA clean, self-sustaining Ignition (Q shown as ≈ ∞), with the largest net export (several hundred MW).
StressPushed past the beta limit into Disruption Risk (high fuel, weaker field).
ShutdownEmergency quench on; fusion power near zero.

First net-positive preset: Cruise. A reactor can make fusion power yet export nothing because the plant’s own recirculating power (magnets, pumps, and especially plasma heating) must be paid first; net power needs a high Q, not merely Q > 1.

3Lawson operating point

  • Ignition target ≈ 3 ×10²¹ keV·s·m⁻³. “Fraction of the way” = their triple product ÷ 3.
  • Below the ignition curve until they approach ignition; the High Gain preset puts the point on/above it.
  • Raising B and T moves the point up and to the right. Stronger field raises confinement time τE, which raises n·T·τE; higher T raises the reaction rate, and both push toward the ignition curve.

4Predict, then test

a. Raise magnetic field → τE increases (a stronger field holds the plasma’s energy longer), so the triple product and Q rise.
b. High fuel + weak field → a disruption. Pressure (high beta) outruns what the field can hold, and confinement is lost.
c. Divertor sweep → peak wall heat flux drops. Sweeping the strike point spreads the exhaust load over a larger area.

5Mission pass-conditions

Exact thresholds the simulator checks (any state that satisfies them counts).

MissionPasses when…

The three on the worksheet (First Light, Net Positive, Ignition) form a natural arc: make the reaction happen, make it pay, then make it self-sustaining. A reliable route to Ignition is the High Gain preset, then trimming fuel below 75%.

7Short-answer model responses

a. 80/20 split. The alpha particles (≈20%) stay in the plasma and heat it; the neutrons (≈80%) escape into the blanket, where their heat ultimately makes the electricity. Ignition depends on the alpha fraction: once alpha self-heating alone replaces the losses, the burn sustains itself and external heating can fall toward zero.
b. Tritium breeding ratio (TBR). Tritium produced in the lithium blanket per tritium burned. Tritium isn’t available naturally in useful amounts, so a power plant must breed its own fuel; a TBR above 1.0 means it replaces (slightly more than) what it consumes.
c. Disruptions. Plasma pressure outruns the confining magnetic field (the beta limit), confinement collapses, and the stored energy dumps onto the wall. Students avoid it by raising the magnetic field and/or easing fuel before the disruption-risk lamp climbs.
