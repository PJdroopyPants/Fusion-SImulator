00

What is already excellent

Said plainly because the rest of this document is corrective by nature: this is a genuinely accomplished piece of work, well past the level most outreach demos ever reach. The recommendations are refinements, not rescues.

  • A real dependency-free 3D engine on a 2D canvas — perspective camera, painter's depth sort, fog, bloom, vignette — with a raymarched WebGL plasma core that falls back safely to software when WebGL is missing. That fallback is exactly the right instinct for a booth.
  • A physically motivated model: triple product, a real Lawson ignition curve, energy gain Q, a power balance, breeding ratio, and genuine burn-through dynamics where alpha self-heating can run away into ignition or collapse.
  • Outstanding teaching content: tiered tech/plain lessons, a context-aware coach, seven missions, predict-then-test, a glossary, a guided story, and a tour — all written clearly and accurately.
  • Serious accessibility groundwork: reduced-motion paths, four themes including high-contrast and colorblind-safe, ARIA live regions on the readouts, keyboard reset, touch gestures, and per-slider steppers.
  • Clean, disciplined code: no debug leftovers, bounded particle and history buffers, sensible persistence of theme/units/sound, and a kiosk mode with an idle auto-demo.

03

Visual form & first impression

A booth is a hostile place for software: bright rooms, a few seconds of attention, and no one reading a manual. The visuals already carry the spectacle; these items remove the small frictions that quietly undercut it.

Confirm the default theme

A1

The app boots in high-contrast — <html data-theme="contrast"> in the markup, and localStorage.getItem("fusionTheme") || "contrast" in the script — yet your README and the theme dropdown both lead with "Control room" (dark) as the signature look.

Why it matters: the first palette a stranger sees is currently the flattest one, not the cinematic one you designed around. If high-contrast was a deliberate projector choice, keep it; otherwise default to dark and leave contrast one click away.

Hook: change the fallback in the theme-init read and the root attribute; everything else is already variable-driven.
Quick winEffort · LowAll

"NTTE" — the τ is lost to uppercasing

A2

Your central figure of merit displays as NTTE and confinement as TE. The markup is correct (nT&tau;E), but .metric-label{text-transform:uppercase} turns the Greek τ into a Latin-looking capital T. The glossary, which is not uppercased, renders τ correctly — proof the cause is the transform.

Why it matters: the notation is the lesson. A physics tool that mangles n·T·τE on its headline card undercuts its own credibility for the undergrad audience.

Hook: drop the uppercase on these two labels, or wrap the symbol in a span with text-transform:none.
Bug / defectEffort · LowUG

Empty voids from locked panel heights

A3

The Plasma Core lesson panel, the Power Balance panel, and the Output-Over-Time panel each show large blank areas because their height is pinned to a taller neighbor in the same grid row. The lesson panel in particular leaves roughly a third of its height empty below the note.

Why it matters: empty boxes read as "unfinished" at a glance. These voids are also opportunities: the lesson panel could host its formula or a small diagram; the Power Balance void is the natural home for your roadmap's step-through energy ledger (C10).

Hook: let these panels size to content (align-items:start on the grid), or fill the space with content you already compute.
Quick winEffort · LowAll

Controls hidden below an internal scrollbar

A4

.sim-grid > .panel is fixed at height: calc(100vh - 150px), so on a typical laptop the Control Room's four toggles — neutral beam, pellet pacing, divertor sweep, emergency quench — sit below an internal scroll a visitor may never notice. The same applies to the lower telemetry.

Why it matters: a booth visitor who never scrolls inside a panel simply never finds the divertor or quench — two of your most teachable controls.

Hook: a more compact control stack, a two-column toggle/slider layout at shorter viewport heights, or a subtle "more below" fade affordance.
High impactEffort · MedAll

The cold-open shows a losing reactor

A5

On the default Startup preset the three headline badges read Burning Plasma · −265 MW · Q 1.6. It is physically honest (sub-breakeven), but a passerby reads a big red negative number as "broken."

Why it matters: the first 1–2 seconds decide whether someone stops. Consider opening at a gentler just-below-breakeven point, labeling the state "Warming up," or letting the kiosk idle-demo reach a net-positive beat quickly.

Hook: tune the startup entry in presetValues, or seed the first load from a "warm start" preset.
Quick winEffort · LowHS / public

Typography micro-nits

A6

At ignition the gain reads "Q = ≈ ∞" — the "=" and "≈" together are redundant; "Q ≈ ∞" is cleaner. In the Sankey, the small "heat 20 + fusion 1890" sublabels crowd the "Power in" node and can overlap at some widths.

Why it matters: these are the polish details that separate "student project" from "exhibit." Tiny fixes, disproportionate credibility.

Hook: build qBig as Q ${qStr} when qStr already starts with "≈"; give the Sankey node labels a min-gap or hide sublabels under a width threshold.
Quick winEffort · LowAll
04

Physics realism & teaching depth

The model is already well-judged for intuition. These items raise its honesty for engineering undergraduates and protect it for whoever maintains it after the handoff.

Decouple the sim clock from a fixed dt

B1

The model tick runs setInterval(() => { advanceDynamics(0.36); … }, 360) and always advances exactly 0.36 simulated seconds. But timers coalesce on a busy machine and throttle hard in a background tab, so simulated time silently drifts from the wall clock — and the "last ~40 s" history axis stops being true.

Why it matters: the burn-through ramp is one of your best teaching moments; it should feel the same on a fast laptop and a cheap tablet. Your render loop already measures real delta — the model loop should too.

Hook: in the interval, compute elapsed seconds from performance.now(), clamp to a sane max (say 0.5 s), and pass that to advanceDynamics and the materials/history updates.
Quick winEffort · LowUG

Reactivity caption vs. chart label

B2

The D-T Reactivity caption says the rate "peaks near 65 keV," while the chart annotation reads "peak ≈ 69 keV." Both are in the right neighborhood, but the two numbers disagree on the same screen.

Why it matters: a sharp undergrad will notice, and it is a 30-second fix. Pick one value (the D-T ⟨σv⟩ maximum is around 64–66 keV) and use it in both places.

Hook: the caption lives in index.html; the label is drawn in drawReactivity().
Bug / defectEffort · LowUG

Add a visible "model card"

B3

The physics is principled but its assumptions are implicit. A short, optional panel would make them teachable: the gain closure Q = 5φ/(1−φ), the 20/80 alpha/neutron split, the ignition curve L(T), the τE ∝ B^1.4 scaling, and one honest line that it is calibrated for intuition, not engineering.

Why it matters: for educators and undergraduates, credibility comes from knowing exactly where the simplifications are. It also turns "magic numbers" into a discussion prompt.

Hook: a new collapsible panel in the learning row, same pattern as the glossary; the relations already live in calculateModel() and the header comment.
High impactEffort · MedUG / educators

Guard the model with a self-check

B4

You intend a student to be able to maintain this. A tiny test harness — reachable at, say, ?selftest — could assert the invariants the physics must keep: stronger B raises τE; a 50/50 D-T blend maximizes the rate; emergency quench drives fusion toward zero; each preset lands in its intended state band.

Why it matters: it is cheap insurance that a well-meaning future edit cannot silently break the model in a way no one notices until a booth.

Hook: a small function that runs calculateModel() across a grid of inputs and logs pass/fail; no framework, no dependency.
High impactEffort · MedUG / maintainer

Optional depth that closes loops you already hint at

B5

Two refinements would reward the curious without bloating the model: (1) helium-ash accumulation that dilutes the fuel and must be exhausted by the divertor — finally making the divertor consequential to the burn; (2) a radiative-loss term (bremsstrahlung rising with temperature and density) so there is a real "too cold / too dilute" lower branch rather than a monotonic climb.

Why it matters: your copy already gestures at both ("removes helium ash that would otherwise quench the burn"), so modeling them makes the words true and deepens the UG exercise.

Hook: evolve an ash fraction state alongside burnT; subtract a Prad term in the power balance.
AmbitiousEffort · MedUG
05

Booth performance & robustness

A donated exhibit has to survive a full open-house day on whatever hardware the host has — often a mid-range tablet. This is the area with the single highest-leverage fix in the whole review.

The one to do first

C1 — stop redrawing everything every frame. animate() calls drawReactor, drawGizmo, drawHistory, drawLawson, drawReactivity, drawSankey, drawFuelCycle, and drawCrossSection on every requestAnimationFrame (~60 fps). But the six analysis/physics charts only change on the 360 ms model tick, and most sit far below the fold. On an all-day booth tablet this is the dominant battery and heat cost, for no visible benefit.

Draw the slow charts only when they change

C1

In order of payoff: (a) mark the six charts "dirty" only on the model tick and on resize, and skip them otherwise; (b) wrap each off-screen canvas in an IntersectionObserver and skip it while not visible; (c) keep only the reactor stage on the per-frame path, optionally capped near 40 fps.

Why it matters: realistically a 3–5× cut in idle CPU/GPU, which on a tablet means less throttling, less fan, and a screen that still looks crisp at hour six.

Hook: a dirty flag set in the setInterval tick; gate each chart's draw call on it plus an isVisible flag.
High impactEffort · MedAll

Pause when the tab or screen sleeps

C2

There is no visibilitychange handling. When the kiosk display sleeps or the tab is backgrounded, the 360 ms interval keeps firing (throttled), advancing the fixed-dt clock out of step (see B1), and the rAF loop resumes mid-state.

Why it matters: pausing both loops on hide and resuming cleanly on show saves power and keeps the model coherent across a long unattended day.

Hook: on document.hidden, cancel the rAF and clear the interval; restore on focus.
Quick winEffort · LowAll

A "lite" path for weak GPUs

C3

Every .panel and the reactor stage use backdrop-filter: blur(14px), recomposited each frame. It is gorgeous on a laptop and a real cost on integrated tablet graphics.

Why it matters: the same instinct that gave you the reduceMotion path applies to GPU load. Auto-detect a weak device (low hardwareConcurrency, or an FPS probe that dips) and drop blur, simplify bloom, and thin the particle counts.

Hook: a body.lite class that zeroes the blur and the heavy passes; toggle it from a one-time FPS sample.
High impactEffort · MedAll

Survive an unattended day

C4

Two safeguards for a walk-away exhibit: an idle watchdog that resets to a friendly preset after a few minutes of no input, so no one ever walks up to a reactor the last visitor left mid-disruption; and WebGL context-loss recovery (webglcontextlost/restored) so a long session can't end on a black plasma. Your software fallback covers a missing context, but not one lost mid-run.

Why it matters: these are exactly the failure modes that only appear after hours, i.e. only at the actual event.

Hook: reuse the kiosk idle timer for the reset; add the two canvas event listeners next to initPlasmaGL().
High impactEffort · MedAll

Already in good shape

Memory hygiene looks sound — sparks cap at 90, the trail at 26, history at 120. Worth one deliberate multi-hour soak test to confirm nothing accumulates across re-inits (stray listeners, audio nodes), but I found no obvious leak in the code.

"Make it a gift" packaging

These are the pieces that turn a great simulator into a donation a program can adopt and feel ownership of. Three are straight from your own roadmap (D2–D4) and simply aren't built yet; the rest are small.

Credits & donation panel

D1

A small "About this exhibit" footer or modal naming the gift to the program, with room for their logos and links and a one-line model disclaimer.

Why it matters: it makes the gift feel official and gives the host program a sense of ownership — the difference between "a thing we found" and "our exhibit."

Hook: your existing disclaimer copy is a good seed; reuse the panel/lesson styling.
Quick winEffort · LowAll

QR takeaway card

D2

When a visitor hits a milestone, offer a printable / downloadable card — "I reached Q = X at the fusion booth" — with a QR to the program's fusion page.

Why it matters: it converts a 90-second interaction into a follow-up, which is the entire point of outreach. This is the highest-ROI new feature for recruiting.

Hook: render the card to an offscreen canvas from the current model (you already export the run), then offer it as a PNG; a tiny vendored QR generator keeps it offline.
High impactEffort · MedAll

Lab worksheet + instructor key

D3

The CSV export exists; the paired worksheet does not. A one-page handout — derive the Lawson point, complete three missions, export the run, interpret it — plus an instructor answer key for the missions and the predict-then-test questions, drops the sim straight into a Rose-Hulman lab period.

Why it matters: curriculum fit is what makes a tool get used after the open house, not just admired at it.

Hook: author it as a short PDF/markdown beside the app; the reading-level switch lets one file serve both an open house and a sophomore lab.
High impactEffort · MedUG

Packaging polish

D4

No favicon, no branded document head beyond the title, and no web-app manifest. For a tablet kiosk, a manifest plus an apple-touch-icon enables "Add to Home Screen" full-screen launch with no browser chrome — a genuine booth win — and a favicon just makes it feel finished.

Why it matters: these are minutes of work that change how "done" the gift feels, and the manifest meaningfully improves the kiosk experience.

Hook: add a small inline-SVG favicon, a manifest.webmanifest with display:"fullscreen", and an author/license meta line.
Quick winEffort · LowAll

Deep-linkable operating points

D5

Serialize the current setpoints into the URL hash so a state can be shared or bookmarked. An instructor could hand out a link (or QR) that opens the sim at an exact scenario, and the worksheet could reference precise starting points.

Why it matters: no backend, pure win — it makes the worksheet (D3) and the QR card (D2) far more powerful, and lets you pre-stage a booth demo.

Hook: read/write a compact hash in applyPreset and on load; you already have the full input set in controls.
High impactEffort · MedAll

A one-page facilitator guide

D6

A short BOOTH-SETUP note: how to launch full-screen, which preset to leave it idling on, how to reset, and a 30-second script a volunteer can read to a visitor.

Why it matters: the people staffing the booth often aren't the people who built it. A half-page makes them confident in minutes.

Hook: a markdown file in the folder; pairs with the watchdog reset (C4) and kiosk mode.
Quick winEffort · LowAll

Accessibility, to finish what you started

Your accessibility work is already a cut above — reduced-motion, four themes, live regions, steppers. Two gaps remain, and closing them matters for a campus that will care about it.

Make the charts readable to a screen reader

E1

The canvases carry aria-labels, but the numbers inside them — the live operating point, the power-balance split, the peak net — are invisible to assistive tech. The telemetry readouts are already in live regions; extend that same pattern to the visualizations.

Why it matters: a visually-hidden text summary or small data table per chart, updated with the model, makes the analysis genuinely usable non-visually.

Hook: a .visually-hidden live region beside each chart, written in the same tick that draws it.
High impactEffort · MedAll

Keyboard control of the 3D stage

E2

Orbit, zoom, and pan are mouse/touch only. The ISO/TOP/FRONT/SIDE snaps and the R reset are a partial path, but there is no arrow-key orbit or +/− zoom when the canvas has focus.

Why it matters: the reactor is the centerpiece; a keyboard-only visitor should be able to look around it too.

Hook: on a focusable canvas, map arrows to cam.yaw/pitch and +/− to zoom — the same state the drag handlers already mutate.
High impactEffort · MedAll
08

A pre-donation QA checklist

Independent of the enhancements above, run this pass before handing it over. It is the difference between "works on my machine" and "works at the event."

  • Open index.html directly from the folder (file://) with no server, in current Chrome, Edge, Firefox, and Safari.
  • Run on a real mid-range tablet for at least an hour: watch for heat, throttling, and frame-rate decay (this is what C1–C3 protect against).
  • Project it in a bright room and read every label from across a room — this is the real test of the theme default (A1) and the τ legibility (A2).
  • Drive each preset and each mission to completion; confirm the state labels, lamps, and coach text all agree.
  • Toggle reduced-motion at the OS level and confirm the stilled experience is still coherent.
  • Do a keyboard-only and a screen-reader pass (E1, E2).
  • Re-verify the Section-06 real-machine figures at donation time — they were current mid-2026 but ITER/SPARC/EAST move.
  • Leave it idle for 10 minutes and confirm it returns to a friendly state (after C4).

A suggested order of attack

Bugs and quick wins first — several change the first impression for the cost of an afternoon — then the one big robustness fix, then the gift pieces, then optional depth.

An afternoonFix what reads as wrong

A2 (τ label), B2 (65 vs 69 keV), A6 (typography), A1 (theme default), A3 (empty panels), A5 (cold-open), C2 (visibility guard), D4 (favicon + manifest). All low-effort, several high-visibility.

A few daysMake it booth-tough & giftable

C1 (per-frame redraw — do this one), C3 (lite GPU path), C4 (watchdog + context loss), A4 (controls below the fold), then the gift trio D1 (credits), D2 (QR card), D3 (worksheet + key), and D5 (deep links).

When you want depthReward the curious

B3 (model card), B4 (self-check), E1/E2 (chart a11y + keyboard orbit), and finally the optional B5 physics refinements. None are required to donate; all raise the ceiling.

Three things to protect

Portability — keep it openable from one folder with no network. Accessibility — keep the reduced-motion, contrast, and colorblind paths first-class as you add features. Performance — gate every new heavy addition behind the same idle/visibility/lite checks, so it stays smooth on the weakest machine in the room.
